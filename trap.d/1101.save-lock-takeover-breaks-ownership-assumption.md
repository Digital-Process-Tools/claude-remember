Gate-3 release audit finding (round 2, v0.45.0..HEAD, dispatch token 7595cbd54f908004)
against the v0.45.0 release (#1115), logged as a trap.d/ fragment per the non-blocking
routing rule in skills/manager/phases/findings.md -- ranking-table row `fails-to-preserve`,
"can ship behind a trap.d fragment".

## What

scripts/save-session.sh carries two comments that assume a lock holder is never displaced
while it still holds the lock:

- lines ~1590-1592: "It cannot steal the lock from a live save either -- steal needs a dead
  pid, adoption needs no pid at all -- so losing the wait means someone is genuinely holding
  it."
- line ~1213: the `now.md.append-*` sweep is described as "Safe: ... it runs under
  save.lock".

#1101 (e704ea2) added exactly the takeover those comments say cannot happen: lib-lock.sh's
`_lock_try_steal` (around lines 533-534, aged empty/non-numeric pid) and a live-holder
takeover gated on `_LOCK_MAX_HOLD_SECONDS` (1800s, around lines 551-552, `elif kill -0 ...`).
Both comments are now false, and the two sections that trusted them -- the sweep and the
copy-then-rename at save-session.sh lines ~1229-1240 and ~1669-1704 (the NDC commit) -- never
re-check that they still own the lock after resuming from any wait. There is no fencing
token.

## Impact

If an original holder resumes after being displaced (a save blocked on I/O for >30 minutes,
or hitting the fast-fail-read path logged in trap.d/1101-1103.lock-read-fast-fail-not-stolen.md),
its copy-then-rename over now.md can overlap with the new holder's own commit. One commit's
entry is then overwritten by the other's `mv`, with no detection. This shares its underlying
cause and its consequence (a lost now.md entry) with the fast-fail-read finding already filed
as PR #1118, but is a distinct trigger: that one is about a pid read misreading a live holder
as dead; this one is about neither of the two critical sections re-validating ownership once a
takeover of any kind becomes possible at all.

## Why this is not blocking (fails-to-preserve, not destroys)

Same tie-break reasoning as the sibling finding: the fix belongs in lock ownership checking
(re-validate before the `mv`, or use a fencing token), not in the destructive `mv` call
itself, and the source transcript genuinely still exists even though the now.md entry built
from it is lost. `gate3_disposition.py --round 2 --verdict findings --blocking no` ->
`carry-forward-and-proceed`.

## What would settle whether this blocks a future release

Whether a save holding save.lock can realistically resume after being displaced -- the
summarize call's own timeout bounds the ordinary case, so only a holder stuck on I/O reaches
the 1800s live-holder takeover window, and the fast-fail-read path (PR #1118) is a separate,
more easily reached trigger for the same underlying gap.

## Fix direction

Re-check lock ownership (e.g. a fencing token written at acquire time and verified immediately
before each destructive `mv`) in both the `now.md.append-*` sweep and the NDC commit path, and
correct the now-false comments at save-session.sh lines ~1213 and ~1590-1592.
