tests/test_is_uint_helper_898.py extracted `_remember_is_uint` from scripts/log.sh with
`^name\(\) \{.*?^\}$|^name\(\) \{[^\n]*\}$` under DOTALL. The helper is a one-liner, but the
regex engine tries the FIRST alternative first, and the lazy `.*?^\}$` happily ran from the
one-liner down to the next line that is exactly `}` -- 209 lines of log.sh (config_into calls,
lib-clock source, the whole log() function). The test then compared "the helper" against the
old guard while executing all of that; on macOS it still printed the right verdict (with
"command not found" on stderr nobody read), on windows-latest it printed nothing at all for
the empty input and failed `assert 'bad' == ''`.

Cost: one Windows failure that looked like a Git Bash quirk in the helper, plus the time to
find that the helper never ran alone. Confirmed by printing the extracted text (209 lines) and
by the new positive control, which fails on the old regex (stderr not empty, `log` defined
alongside the helper) and passes on the reordered one.

General shape: when an extraction regex has a "one-line form | block form" alternation, the
specific form goes first, and something should assert the extracted text is only the thing.
